# Warframe systems and top-down/isometric level design: lessons for a 3/4 top-down sci-fi action looter in Godot

Scope note: (A) WARFRAME's procedural tilesets, mission types, mods, abilities, movement and loot; (B) level-design practice in Diablo I-IV, Path of Exile, Hades, Enter the Gungeon, Hyper Light Drifter, Ruiner, The Ascent (Helldivers yielded nothing usable, see Gaps), plus hybrid procedural techniques that work in Godot. Every claim cites its source. Points under "Inferences" are my own reasoning and are marked as such. Community wikis (Warframe wiki, Hades fandom, Diablo fandom) are player-edited and can lag patches. The saschb2b "game-mechanics" pages are a third-party analysis site, and their numbers are approximations.

---

## Q1. Warframe tileset system: how hand-made tiles are assembled procedurally, connectors, tile counts, variety

### Takeaway
Warframe levels are hand-built rooms ("tiles") from one themed tileset, joined door-to-door into a tree with no loops. A per-mission-type template decides the sequence and mix of tile roles (Start / Connector / Intermediate / Objective / Dead-end / Exit), the segment lengths and how deep branches can go. Variety comes from (1) many tiles per set (30 or more), (2) mission templates that reshape the same tiles, and (3) AI-side pacing (tactical and influence maps that spawn enemies ahead of the player and ramp intensity), not from per-tile scripting.

### Cited Findings
- Tilesets are procedurally generated environments built from "predefined rooms or tiles" joined by "compatible 'doors' or 'portals'". Landscapes (open worlds) are the exception. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- "A tile set consists of at least 30 different tiles, which are rooms of varying sizes." — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Layout topology: "A typical mission will always generate tiles in a tree-like structure with no overlap." No circular paths are generated, and side branches always end in dead ends. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Tile roles: Start (S), Connector (C), Intermediate (I), Objective (O), Dead end (D, which usually holds storage containers and lockers) and Exit (E), plus Cube map / Skybox backdrops. A layout is logged as a string such as `SCICOCICE`. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Each mission type and tileset pair uses a developer-defined template. The template sets "what tiles are available", each tile's type, "the length and tile composition of each map segment", and "the maximum branching depth for creating offshoot paths". — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- The same tileset plays differently under different templates. Exterminate on Corpus Gas City is more linear, with more Connector tiles. Survival on Grineer Galleon is more maze-like, with more Intermediate tiles for life-support towers and choke points. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- There are currently 25 unique tilesets, and each is restricted to its own theme (Gas City missions use only Gas City tiles). The game writes each generated layout to EE.log: the segment string plus the level file used for each tile. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Environmental and dynamic content lives inside tiles: sub-zero temperatures that drain shields, fire that damages and alerts enemies, moving cargo containers, traps, parkour areas, zip lines and randomly placed explosives. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Open worlds ("Landscapes": Plains of Eidolon, Orb Vallis, Cambion Drift, Duviri) "do not use procedurally-generated segments, but instead use expansive, seamlessly fixed environments". — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Production pipeline for the Corpus Ice Planet tileset (2014): the art director writes a short art bible (backstory, mood, palette, real-world references). Tech artists then prototype weather in test levels. Then a layout artist receives each portion "in a very 'bare bones' state" and builds the levels. A lighting artist starts once design and art are "close to 90%" done. Output target: the tileset "should be able to produce anywhere between 40-45 level variations." — [Warframe: Building the Ice Planet](https://www.warframe.com/news/building-warframe-ice-planet)
- GDC 2013 (Daniel Brewer, lead AI programmer at DE): with procedural levels "the usual level design tricks won't work". Map sections can repeat, so designers cannot hand-place spawn points, and scripted sequences would become repetitive. — [MCV: GDC 13 handling AI in procedural levels](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/gdc-13-handling-ai-in-procedural-levels/)
  - **Tac map**: distance to objectives and key locations. The game raises enemy count and strength as the squad nears the objective, so action builds to a peak at the end.
  - **Influence map**: tracks player line of sight and movement direction "to ensure enemies spawned in front of players".
  - **Intensity metric**: kills and damage taken, normalized by player level. Intensity peaks, is held briefly, then eases off, giving a "roller coaster" that mimics scripted pacing. — [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/gdc-13-handling-ai-in-procedural-levels/)
- According to a GamesRadar retrospective (search snippet only, not fetched), the core generator "hasn't changed too much; it just has many more pieces of pipe to work with". Growth came from adding tilesets and factions, plus hand-built open worlds. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/warframes-tough-to-start-but-thats-also-why-its-still-so-fun)
- Spy vaults are hand-designed puzzle rooms inside the tileset: "Each tileset is generated from existing vault designs, and individual features are randomly altered." Alternate routes and tunnels can bypass defenses, and some passages (grates) are randomly impassable in a given mission. — [Warframe Wiki: Spy](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Spy)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Warframe's recipe for "feels hand-made, but replayable": **authored rooms + a role-typed slot grammar + runtime AI pacing**. The level never needs to know about combat beats, because the spawn director supplies them (enemies ahead of the player, intensity peaks near the objective). That makes it the cheapest route to a good-feeling procedural mission and fits a small team.
- (Inference) The tree-with-dead-ends topology is fine for horde shooters with fast movement (dead ends hold loot, so a detour is a choice). It does produce backtracking, though. For a top-down game, consider adding one loop per mission, or putting the extraction near the end of the tree, so players don't walk back through cleared tiles (compare the Diablo IV backtracking fix in Q4).
- (Inference) For Godot: one `PackedScene` per tile, with `Marker3D` "door" nodes that carry socket type and size. A template resource is a string or array of roles (`S C I O C I E`) plus branch depth and length ranges. Log the layout string exactly like EE.log, so a bad seed can be reproduced.
- (Inference) The "40-45 level variations" figure suggests DE thinks of a tileset as a content budget of about 30 tiles that yields a few dozen meaningfully distinct maps, not infinite variety. Players learn the tiles, and learning them is part of the appeal (see Hades below).

### Gaps
- No primary DE talk was found giving exact tile dimensions, door-socket standards, or the per-tileset tile count beyond "at least 30". No DE source on how loops or overlaps are prevented algorithmically (only the wiki's statement that there are none).
- The GamesRadar quote comes from a search snippet and was not verified in full text.

## Q2. Warframe mission types and how each changes play; open world vs missions

### Takeaway
Warframe reuses the same tiles and enemies across 30+ objective "verbs", and each verb changes how the player moves through the space. Kill-everything (Exterminate) means sweeping the whole tree. Speed-and-stealth (Spy, Rescue, Capture) means routing and avoiding alarms. Holding a point (Defense, Mobile Defense, Interception) turns one tile into an arena. Endless timers with escalating difficulty and periodic reward rotations (Survival, Defense, Excavation) let the player choose how long to push. This is the single most transferable idea: one tile library, many objectives.

### Cited Findings
- Over 30 mission types exist, split into standard and endless. Survival, Defense, Disruption, Excavation and Defection are endless, "where the enemies will scale with time and/or player equipment ranking". Players finish objectives and then extract via Landing Craft. — [Warframe Wiki: Mission](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mission)
- Objective summaries (secondary guide): — [Gamepressure: Warframe mission types](https://www.gamepressure.com/warframe-game-guide-and-walkthrough/mission-types/z6acb0)
  - Exterminate: kill a set number of enemies; short runs.
  - Capture: catch a fleeing target (it can outrun you and use smoke), then extract; "a very fast mission".
  - Spy: extract data from 3 servers quietly; an alarm starts a timer.
  - Defense: hold a point against waves; every 5 waves you choose to stay or leave, and you get a reward.
  - Mobile Defense: carry a datamass to consoles and defend each while it uploads.
  - Survival: endless; oxygen depletes; collect life-support capsules or enemy drops; reward every 5 minutes.
  - Sabotage: find and destroy an object; usually quick.
  - Rescue: free a hostage and escort them to extraction; stealth recommended; alerting enemies starts a countdown to the hostage's death.
  - Interception: hold capture points until you reach 100% map control.
  - Excavation: defend drills powered by power cells that enemies drop; reward per successful excavation.
  - Hijack: escort a vehicle that only moves while its shield is up.
  - Assassination: boss kill; one per planet; drops blueprints.
  - Defection: escort people during Infested attacks.
- Spy details: 3 Data Vaults; at least 1 hack must succeed. Tripping security starts a 20-50 s countdown (difficulty-dependent) before the data is wiped. Each alarm makes later vaults harder. Hacking without any alarm triples the affinity. If all three alarms trigger, the mission becomes an Exterminate. Reward rotation depends only on how many vaults are hacked. Vault defenses vary by faction: Corpus cameras and laser barriers (some move or periodically shut off); Grineer sensor bars and motion detectors; Orokin Void Rifts, mobile sentries and laser traps. — [Warframe Wiki: Spy](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Spy)
- Survival numbers: life support drains "1% per 1.5 seconds" (0% in about 150 s without refills). Kills can drop personal modules (+5%). Capsules drop every 90 s, with a 30 s warning, and restore 30% each (max 8 on the map). Extraction and the first reward unlock at 5 min. The reward rotation repeats A, A, B, C every 20 min. Eximus (elite) spawn chance rises from 0% at 5 min to 20% at 60 min. Enemy unit types unlock over time (e.g., Grineer Heavy Gunner at 15+ min). — [Warframe Wiki: Survival](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Survival)
- Defense reward tiers unlock "at each 3rd wave checkpoint". There are three tiers. — [Warframe Wiki: Mission](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mission)
- Template shape follows mission type: Survival on Galleon uses more Intermediate tiles for "life support towers and choke points". Exterminate on Gas City is more linear. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Interception mostly uses Defense tiles. — [Warframe Wiki: Mission](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mission)
- Open worlds (Landscapes) are fixed, seamless maps, not procedural. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Mission verbs sort into four spatial patterns that a top-down game can reuse on the same room library:
  1. **Sweep**: Exterminate or Sabotage. Linear template, kill-count or target at the end.
  2. **Route-under-pressure**: Spy, Rescue, Capture. Hand-made "vault" rooms with alarms, plus a fail timer. Rewards stealth or speed, and the alarm escalates the remaining rooms.
  3. **Hold**: Defense, Mobile Defense, Interception, Excavation. Needs a few authored "arena" tiles with 3-4 spawn approaches, cover and a defend object. Mobile Defense chains 2-3 holds across the map, which forces movement between fights.
  4. **Endure**: Survival. A resource timer (life support) forces players to roam and kill rather than camp. Escalation plus a periodic stay-or-leave choice (5 min or 5 waves) creates push-your-luck pacing.
- (Inference) Survival's life-support drain is an elegant anti-camping device: the player must keep killing to refill, and capsules (fixed refills every 90 s) pull the player across tiles. Cheap to copy in Godot with a timer and pickups.
- (Inference) "Alarm converts the mission" (Spy becoming Exterminate) is a cheap way to make failure produce a different, still-fun mission rather than a hard fail.
- (Inference) A/A/B/C rotations put the best reward at the 20-minute or 20-wave mark, which drives long stays and repeat runs. For a looter, this is a lever on session length.

### Gaps
- No primary DE design commentary was found on why each mission type exists. Descriptions come from the wiki and a secondary guide. Capture and Rescue timers, and Mobile Defense terminal counts, were not confirmed numerically.

## Q3. Warframe mods (capacity, polarity, behavior vs stat mods), abilities and energy, movement and why speed matters, loot (relics, drop tables)

### Takeaway
Warframe builds are a constrained "deck" of mods. A capacity budget (30 points, doubled to 60 by a Reactor or Catalyst) and slot polarities (a matching polarity halves the cost) force trade-offs, and Forma re-polarizing is the long-term sink. Most mods are stat lines. The memorable ones (augments, corrupted dual-stat mods, rivens) change behavior or trade one stat for another. Movement (bullet jump at 14.5 m/s, wall latch, aim glide, slide, a roll with 75% damage reduction) lets players cross large procedural tiles fast. Loot runs through layered tables: mission rotations drop relics, and relics roll 6 slots at 3 rarity tiers with refinement upgrades.

### Cited Findings
**Mods**
- Capacity: 15 at unranked, up to 30 (40 for some items) with item rank. An Orokin Reactor (Warframes) or Catalyst (weapons) **doubles** capacity. — [Warframe Wiki: Mods](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mods)
- Drain 0-20 per mod (e.g., Serration costs 14). A matching polarity halves drain, rounded up (Serration costs 7 in a Madurai slot). A mismatched polarity adds about 25%. Aura, Stance and Posture mods *add* capacity; matching doubles the bonus, mismatching cuts it by 25%. — [Warframe Wiki: Mods](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mods)
- Slots: Warframes have 8 general + 1 Exilus (utility/mobility) + 1 Aura. Primary and secondary weapons have 8 + Exilus. Melee has 8 + Exilus + Stance. Companions have 10, Necramechs 12. — [Warframe Wiki: Mods](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mods)
- Fusion: Endo + credits; each rank costs double the previous (10, 20, 40...). Most mods max at rank 10; "Flawed" mods max at 3. — [Warframe Wiki: Mods](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mods)
- Mod kinds: standard single-stat (Serration); dual-stat; **conditional** (trigger in combat); **Corrupted** (one stat up, one down); **Augments** ("change a specific Warframe ability or weapon"); special-slot mods (Aura = squad buff + capacity; Stance = melee combos; Exilus = utility/mobility). — [Warframe Wiki: Mods](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mods)
- Riven mods: up to three random bonuses plus a penalty, scaled by the weapon's "disposition" (weaker weapons get stronger rivens). Veiled until a challenge is completed. Can be re-rolled ("transmutation"). — [Warframe Wiki: Mods](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mods)
- Forma polarizes slots (lowering costs on that slot), which is the long-term investment sink. — [Warframe Wiki: Mods](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Mods)

**Abilities and energy**
- Abilities "at base, cost up to 100 energy to cast". Examples cost 25, 50 and 75. Energy pools are e.g. 100 at rank 0 and 150 at rank 30 (Ash), or 200/300 (Volt Prime). Energy "does not regenerate over time by default". It comes from orbs, abilities and mods. Some frames use alternative resources (Hildryn spends shields; Lavos uses cooldowns). — [Warframe Wiki: Energy](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Energy)
- Ability effects scale through Strength, Duration, Range and Efficiency stats, which mods change (e.g., Thurible's radius grows 13 to 20 m by rank; Razor Gyre refunds a quarter of its cost per enemy hit). — [Warframe Wiki: Energy](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Energy)

**Movement**
- Bullet jump: "initial speed of 14.5 m/s" toward the reticle. Deals 100 Blast damage in 3 m. Not repeatable until the player touches ground; wall dash or wall latch resets it. — [Warframe Wiki: Movement](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Movement)
- Roll: 75% damage reduction and knockdown immunity during the animation. Rolling before landing cancels a hard landing (hard landings trigger at 20 m/s). — [Warframe Wiki: Movement](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Movement)
- Aim glide 3 s and wall latch 6 s share one timer. Double jump recharges on touching any surface. Wall dash needs an approach angle under 70°. Base movement is about 6 m/s (7.5 m/s on ziplines). Per-frame sprint multipliers range from 0.9 to 1.5. Slam jump reaches about 50 m/s. — [Warframe Wiki: Movement](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Movement)
- Sliding is crouching while moving; friction slows the frame except on slopes. Melee during a slide becomes a slide attack. — [Warframe Wiki: Parkour](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Parkour)
- Community view (speedrun guide snippet): after "coptering" (a melee-spin exploit) was removed, "speed is now a necessity". Forum players complained that base run speed is too slow, so bullet jump does most forward travel. — [speedrun.com WF movement guide](https://www.speedrun.com/wf/guide/u2q3n) (search snippet; community opinion)

**Loot: relics and drop tables**
- Void Relics: tiers Lith, Meso, Neo, Axi (plus Requiem). 6 rewards each: "Three common drops, two uncommon drops and one rare drop." Opening one needs 10 Reactant from corrupted enemies during a Fissure mission. — [Warframe Wiki: Void Relic](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Void_Relic)
- Refinement (spend Void Traces): Intact 76/22/2%; Exceptional (25 traces) 70/26/4%; Flawless (50) 60/34/6%; Radiant (100) 50/40/10% for common/uncommon/rare. — [Warframe Wiki: Void Relic](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Void_Relic)
- Squad reward: each player's relic rolls independently, and each player picks one reward from up to four squad results. This creates the cooperative "radshare" (everyone opens the same Radiant relic). — [Warframe Wiki: Void Relic](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Void_Relic)
- Vaulting removes relics from drop tables "to keep Prime part drop rates from being diluted". 759 relics are vaulted. Endless fissures give pre-refined relics every 5th rotation. — [Warframe Wiki: Void Relic](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Void_Relic)

### Inferences
- (Inference) What makes the mod system work: **a scarce budget (capacity) + slot affinity (polarity) + permanent per-item investment (Forma, Reactor)** turns each weapon into a long project. Stat mods are the "floor". Corrupted (trade-off) mods, augments (behavior change) and Exilus utility mods are where identity comes from. For a top-down ARPG, a few slot-typed sockets (an Aura-like squad slot, an Exilus-like mobility/utility slot) plus a capacity cost per mod gives build tension cheaply.
- (Inference) Warframe energy (no passive regen; orbs drop from kills) is a combat-feeding resource loop: kill to cast, cast to kill. That suits a horde action game better than cooldowns alone.
- (Inference) Why speed matters: tiles are large and the mission tree has dead ends, so fast traversal (14.5 m/s bullet jump against about 6 m/s base) keeps the downtime between fights short, and it makes procedural sprawl tolerable. In a 3/4 top-down game, the analog is a dash or slide with i-frames or damage reduction (Warframe's roll gives 75% DR) and a movement-speed stat on gear. Level metrics must then scale up (see Q6: arenas grow with player mobility).
- (Inference) The relic design separates *where you farm* (mission rotation) from *what you get* (relic table), and refinement lets players convert a grind currency into rare odds. It's a good pattern for "targeted farming" in a looter.

### Gaps
- No primary DE source on the design rationale for polarity, or on Parkour 2.0 (Update 17, 2015) goals. The wiki pages fetched did not document the Parkour 2.0 history. The "4 abilities per Warframe" convention is well known, but no fetched page stated it explicitly in this session. Efficiency cap and energy-orb values were not in the fetched portion.

## Q4. Diablo / Path of Exile: how dungeon layouts are generated, sizes, packs, events, pacing

### Takeaway
The ARPG lineage (Diablo I to IV, PoE) uses fixed "puzzle pieces" (tiles or chunks), arranged by rules that guarantee required features: entrance and exit on different edges, fixed relative exit direction, one set piece per level. Hand-made set pieces and theme rooms are dropped into procedural floorplans. Density is tuned statistically over thousands of generated maps, not per-map. Diablo IV's launch fixes show the modern priority: kill backtracking (objectives moved onto the main path, stragglers seek the player) and make events frequent (dungeon event chance raised from 10% to 60%).

### Cited Findings
**Diablo I (technical, from code analysis)**
- 4 stages × 4 levels (Cathedral, Catacombs, Caves, Hell). Levels fill a **40×40** tile grid, and each tile splits into 4 "dungeon pieces" for rendering. A typical map has 100+ distinct tiles, many of them variants for connections. — [Boris the Brave: Dungeon generation in Diablo 1](https://boristhebrave.com/2019/07/14/dungeon-generation-in-diablo-1)
- Two passes: first a "predungeon" floor plan (walkable, doors, high-level details), then tile selection (marching squares or 3×3 pattern matching to pick wall tiles). — [Boris the Brave](https://boristhebrave.com/2019/07/14/dungeon-generation-in-diablo-1)
- Hand-authored injections: **Set pieces** (pre-authored chunks pasted verbatim, one per level at most, mostly for quests); **Minisets** (about 3×3 pattern-matched patches for stairs, join fixes and variation); **Theme rooms** (walled rooms with a door and preset content such as libraries or monster pits, with size and stage requirements); **tile substitutions** (equivalent variants, avoiding nearby repeats). If the dungeon isn't fully walkable, generation restarts. — [Boris the Brave](https://boristhebrave.com/2019/07/14/dungeon-generation-in-diablo-1)
- Cathedral: up to 3 rooms of 10×10 along a central axis, joined by a wide corridor, with rooms budding off (sides 2, 4 or 6 tiles). Central rooms get 4 columns, and the hallway gets a colonnade. Catacombs: recursive subdivision with rooms 4-9 tiles per side and hallways 1-3 tiles wide; restart if under 700 floor tiles. Caves: cellular growth plus erosion; retry if under 600 floor tiles; lava pools from enclosed wall pockets under 40 tiles; up to 4 rivers of 7-100 tiles, each needing a bridge. Hell: a 20×20 quadrant mirrored to full size. — [Boris the Brave](https://boristhebrave.com/2019/07/14/dungeon-generation-in-diablo-1)

**Diablo II**
- Maps are built from tiles: "Think of them as a piece of a big jigsaw puzzle". The pieces are fixed, but their arrangement varies. — [Maxroll: D2 map reading](https://maxroll.gg/d2/resources/map-reading)
- Rule-constrained randomness players can learn. Many dungeons put exits, waypoints and bosses at fixed *relative* directions (e.g., the Forgotten Tower exit is always "Left"). Outdoor areas have a fixed border with constraints: Cold Plains' "2 Exits can't be on the same edge with each other or the Entrance"; Black Marsh's exit "can NEVER be on the bottom left edge". Jungle areas (Spider Forest, Great Marsh, Flayer Jungle) are 2×6 grids of tiles, and in Spider Forest "A River connects all Map tiles". Some maps are fully static (Den of Evil, Tristram, Chaos Sanctuary, Travincal). — [Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/d2/resources/map-reading)
- Monster tiers: normal, champion (sub-types Champion, Berserker, Fanatic, Ghostly, Possessed; boosted level, XP and HP) and unique (gold name, leads a pack with several minions; only one unique per group). — [Diablo Wiki: Champion monsters](https://diablo.fandom.com/wiki/Champion_monsters); [Diablo Wiki: Unique Monsters](https://diablo.fandom.com/wiki/Unique_Monsters) (search snippets). Sources conflict on spawn odds: one says a champion-or-unique pack is a champion 20% of the time, another words it differently. Treat as unverified.

**Diablo IV**
- Dungeon count: the announced figure is **120** ([Gameranx citing Press-Start interview with producer Ash Sweetring](https://gameranx.com/updates/id/419386/article/blizzard-staff-discuss-dungeon-count-and-design-in-diablo-iv)). This conflicts with the pre-launch "150+" ([Push Square, March 2022 dev update](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2022/03/diablo-4-dev-update-talks-of-more-than-150-procedurally-generated-dungeons), search snippet).
- "Tile sets describe what we use in order to build each of the dungeons". Each entry "is going to be a random experience". — Ash Sweetring via [Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/updates/id/419386/article/blizzard-staff-discuss-dungeon-count-and-design-in-diablo-iv)
- Dungeons use "a combination of handcrafted and procedural creation". New "tile-set transition" scenes let one dungeon move seamlessly between two tilesets. — [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2022/3/29/news/131294-diablo-iv-will-feature-150-dungeons-spread-over-5-distinct-zones-and-areas-trailer) (search snippet)
- Dungeon designer Devin Brom: the main challenge is keeping about 150 dungeons fresh. The "twisted, macabre vignettes" come from a narrative core set at the start of each dungeon's design. — [DigiPen](https://www.digipen.edu/showcase/news/devil-details-diablo-iv-dungeon-designer-devin-brom) (search snippet)
- Overworld: Blizzard dropped procedural generation for D4's open world (earlier games "stitched world tiles together"). — [The Loadout](https://www.theloadout.com/diablo-4/sanctuary-blizzard-biggest-gamble) (search snippet)
- **Open-beta retrospective (primary, Blizzard, April 2023):** — [Blizzard: Diablo IV Open Beta Retrospective](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23938289/diablo-iv-open-beta-retrospective-transforming-feedback-into-change)
  - Players felt "they did a lot of backtracking". Layouts were optimized "across all zones", and 9 Fractured Peaks dungeons were reworked. "Many of our Structure Objectives have been repositioned along main dungeon pathways", where before they sat in side rooms.
  - "Small numbers of straggling monsters now seek out the player" to finish Kill-All objectives.
  - Dungeon **Event spawn chance went from 10% to 60%**.
  - Objective friction cut: animus deposit channel 3 s to 0; rescue 3 s to 1.5 s (and it drops a health potion); pedestal channel 2 s to 0; carrying objective items gives +25% move speed ("Momentum"). Returning an object fully restores health, resource and potions. Doors ping the minimap when opened. "All Structure Objectives now have additional combat mechanics."
  - Cellars (mini-dungeons) now always reward a chest and have a guaranteed elite.

**Path of Exile**
- GGG level designers: "there's pockets of designed layout and set pieces that get randomly distributed, but it's still quite controlled" (Tom M). "Rooms do not have controlled entrances and exits, and can be rotated in any direction or even flipped" (Chris G), so pieces must work in any orientation. Designers build "distinct encounter spaces" that are "interchangeable" with restrictions that block bad combinations (Josiah D). — [PoE forum: Interview with our Level Design Department](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1654235)
- Density is tuned by simulation: the team generates thousands of levels and tracks per-area monster and chest counts in a large document to check averages. "Each level has to fit within scope so it's not too bare or too dense." Adding one variant "can tip things in unexpected directions". — [PoE forum interview](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1654235)
- Labyrinth rooms are categorized (combat, trap...) so "it's not just putting a bunch of trap rooms one after another". Fences and gates stop movement skills from skipping traps. — [PoE forum interview](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1654235)
- Chris Wilson (GDC 2019) on sustaining a 13-week release cycle: heavy procedural generation, "multiple axes of randomness", and asset and content reuse. — [80.lv: GDC Designing Path of Exile](https://80.lv/articles/gdc-designing-path-of-exile) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Shared pattern across D1, D2 and PoE: **constraint-based randomness**. Fixed pieces, randomized arrangement, and hard rules for required features (the exit never on the entrance edge; boss at a relative direction). Expert players can then "read" maps, which counts as a feature, not a flaw.
- (Inference) Use D1's two-pass architecture: a coarse logical grid or graph first (rooms, corridors, doors, set-piece slots), then a dressing pass (wall tiles, props, minisets). In Godot this maps to an abstract layout pass, then instancing `GridMap` cells or room `PackedScene`s, then decoration.
- (Inference) Validate every generated level automatically (walkability flood fill, a minimum floor area like D1's 600 to 700 tile thresholds, reachability of every objective), and regenerate on failure. Batch-generate thousands of seeds offline and chart monster and chest counts, as GGG does.
- (Inference) D4's lessons for objective design in procedural dungeons: put objectives on the critical path; give objective-carrying a *bonus* (move speed, full restore) instead of a penalty; remove channel times; send stragglers to the player; make random events common (60%) so most runs contain a surprise.
- (Inference) Pack structure for a looter: normal trash + champion packs (stat-boosted groups) + unique or rare leaders with minions, which gives players visual priority targets. Specific density numbers were not found (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No primary source found for PoE's generator internals (Rhys Abraham's ExileCon talk exists, but no transcript was available: [PoE forum](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2753965)). No sourced numbers for monsters per screen or pack size in D2, D3, D4 or PoE. No sources fetched on shrines or pylons. D2 champion and unique spawn odds conflict across sources.

## Q5. Hades / Enter the Gungeon: room-by-room arena structure, previewed rewards, upgrade choice, waves, run length

### Takeaway
Both games put hand-authored rooms into a short procedural graph. Gungeon uses pre-authored flows (graph templates) with injected special rooms and about 300 hand-made rooms on floor 1. Hades uses a chamber sequence per biome, drawn from a pool of hand-made arenas. In Hades the player steers the run by picking the next door from its reward icon. Its upgrades matter because they are pick-1-of-3 offers from god-themed pools that modify core verbs (attack, special, cast, dash). Rarity and level are two separate axes, and two-god Duo boons and prerequisite-gated Legendaries reward committing to a direction.

### Cited Findings
**Hades structure**
- Hades I has up to 72 chambers per run. Boss chambers sit at 14 (Tartarus), 24 (Asphodel) and 36 (Elysium), not counting Chaos rooms and troves. Most chambers are combat: "Once all waves of enemies have been defeated, the room's reward will appear", and exit doors unlock. — [Hades Wiki: Chambers and Encounters](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Chambers_and_Encounters) (search snippet). Another guide says Tartarus "consists of 15 chambers", a conflict.
- Third-party analysis: each biome draws from a pool of about 30 hand-authored rooms. A run lasts about 20-40 min. Door icons are the only preview: god symbol (boon), anvil (Daedalus Hammer, a weapon upgrade), gold, gems, nectar, heart (healing), Charon's eye (shop) and others. "The player chooses doors based on the reward type they need most." — [saschb2b: Hades chambers and paths](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/hades/chamber-and-paths) (approximate figures)
- Optional side gates: Chaos Gates and Infernal (Erebus) Gates can spawn after normal encounters and skip the next chamber. Erebus is a no-hit challenge that requires Heat 5/10/15 by region. Fountain chambers hold the reward plus a heal. — [GamesRadar: Hades Erebus chambers](https://www.gamesradar.com/hades-erebus-chambers); [Hades Wiki](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Chambers_and_Encounters) (search snippet)
- Supergiant (designer Ed Gorinstein) on arena design per biome:
  - Tartarus: "medium-sized chambers that are almost always completely walled in", to help players learn the Cast and to enable wall-slam damage. Chambers start smaller early in a biome and get bigger later, matching escalating encounters.
  - Asphodel: archipelagos in lava. RTS-style bridges were tried; instead each enemy got its own way to cross magma (hop, float, dash), so the enemies became more mobile. The biome is meant to "feel vast and horizontal".
  - Rejected: gold hidden in shiny walls, and huge rooms with scattered doors. Neither fit the fast room-to-room pace. Discovery instead comes from urns, troves, wells, fishing points and Chaos/Erebus gates.
  - Rooms recombine each run and "rarely wear out their welcome". After about 10 runs, hazards like spike traps are avoided without conscious thought. — [Kotaku: Hades level design is less random than it seems](https://kotaku.com/hades-level-design-is-less-random-than-it-seems-1845254545)
- Kasavin wanted players to spend dozens of hours, "but he wanted to reward them during that time". — [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/hades-changes-what-it-means-to-be-a-roguelike/1100-6483420/) (search snippet)

**Hades boons (why the choices matter)**
- Each boon pickup is "pick 1 of 3" from about 12 Olympian gods (Hades II roster; Hades I has fewer). A 30-minute run ends with about 10 boons. Slots: Attack, Special, Cast, Dash (Hades I also has Call). Each god has a mechanical flavor: Zeus chain lightning, Poseidon knockback, Aphrodite Charm/Weak, Demeter chill, and so on. — [saschb2b: Hades boons and gods](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/hades/boons-and-gods) (third-party)
- Two progression axes: **rarity**, rolled at offer time (Common, Rare about 1.3×, Epic about 1.6×, Heroic about 2×), and **level**, raised by Pom of Power. A Common level 5 can beat a Rare level 1. **Legendary** boons have prerequisites (e.g., Thunder Forge needs 5 Zeus boons in the analysis). **Duo** boons need specific boons from two specific gods held at once (e.g., Lightning Rod = Zeus + Artemis). **Daedalus Hammers** (1 of 2-3, once or twice per run) change how the weapon behaves (e.g., Sister Blades "Quick Spin"). The analysis frames build identity as "a direction in the boon graph the run offered you". — [saschb2b](https://saschb2b.github.io/game-mechanics/games/hades/boons-and-gods) (third-party; multipliers approximate)
- Hades II: Early Access boons that were removed were often "reskinned" or found "new homes" rather than cut outright. — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/hades/hades-2-lead-says-the-best-boons-from-early-access-werent-killed-just-reskinned-we-think-its-important-not-to-be-too-precious-about-these-things) (search snippet)

**Enter the Gungeon structure**
- Each floor picks one of several pre-authored **flows**, graphs with no positions (The Hollow has 4, Gungeon Proper has 8). Each is built around a distinctive feature such as a large loop or a multi-way fork. — [Boris the Brave: Dungeon generation in Enter the Gungeon](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2019/07/28/dungeon-generation-in-enter-the-gungeon/)
- **Injection**: special rooms (extra vendors, jails, fireplace, elevator, secret rooms) are injected with a probability and preconditions. Secret rooms usually sit at dead ends but have a 1/5 chance of attaching to any room, and a 90% chance to appear. — [Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2019/07/28/dungeon-generation-in-enter-the-gungeon/)
- Rooms are hand-authored: "nearly 300" to choose from on floor 1, with no repeats. Room categories: normal (enemies), hub (larger, more exits), connector (no enemies, often a hazard, long and thin), reward, boss, shop, jail, secret, corridor. — [Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2019/07/28/dungeon-generation-in-enter-the-gungeon/)
- Pacing rules: many chests sit behind one-way loops or deep in the level. "The boss room is always a reasonable distance from the start". Enemy rooms are spaced with breather rooms, shops and hubs. — [Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2019/07/28/dungeon-generation-in-enter-the-gungeon/)
- Layout: the flow is split into composites (single loops or trees). The hardest, most central parts (loops) are placed first. Trees are laid out depth-first, with up to 3 backtracking retries. Corridors "must be between 4 and 30 units long (50 in the mines)". — [Boris the Brave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2019/07/28/dungeon-generation-in-enter-the-gungeon/)

### Inferences
- (Inference) **Previewed rewards on doors** are the cheapest way to add agency to a linear procedural chain. Each fork becomes a meaningful decision ("I need a weapon upgrade, not gold"). For a 3/4 top-down sci-fi looter, show reward icons over the exits of each cleared arena (upgrade module, currency, health, shop, elite or challenge room).
- (Inference) Hades upgrades feel meaningful and "upgrade cards" in weaker games don't, because Hades boons (a) **modify verbs**, not just numbers (attack becomes chain lightning; dash leaves a blast); (b) come from **themed pools**, so taking one god's boon raises the odds of synergy with that god's later offers; (c) have **cross-pool payoffs** (Duo boons) and **depth payoffs** (Legendaries gated by count), which reward commitment; (d) have **two upgrade axes** (rarity and level), so later choices remain interesting; (e) are **slot-exclusive** (one Attack boon), so picking one closes a door, which creates real trade-offs. Weak upgrade cards are usually "+10% damage" with no slot competition and no synergy hooks.
- (Inference) Room sizing rule from Hades: small, walled arenas early (teach the basics; walls become a weapon via knockback or wall slams), bigger and more open arenas later, with enemy movement designed for the terrain (Asphodel's lava crossers).
- (Inference) Gungeon's flows are the best model for "hand-made but replayable" structure: author 4-8 graph templates per biome (a loop, a fork, a hub), inject optional rooms with probabilities, fill nodes from a large tagged room pool, and avoid repeats per run.
- (Inference) Wave structure: Hades reveals the reward only after "all waves" are cleared. A 2-3 wave arena with spawns announced by VFX is the standard pattern (see the melee-arena principles in Q6).

### Gaps
- No primary Supergiant source found on boon-pool weighting, the exact Duo count, waves per chamber, or per-biome chamber counts (the wiki and guides conflict: 14 vs 15 for Tartarus). The rarity multipliers come from a third-party analysis and are approximate. No primary Dodge Roll (Gungeon) talk was fetched; the Gungeon analysis is Boris the Brave's reverse-engineering.

## Q6. Top-down / isometric space design: corridor widths, room sizes, cover, height, LOS, chokepoints, flanking, landmarks, verticality, occlusion, camera

### Takeaway
Sourced guidance is thinner here than for generation. The consistent principles: scale arenas to player mobility; keep combat floors mostly flat and open, and avoid waist-high clutter that snags a fast character; bound arenas clearly; give each enemy type the space to do its thing (max about 3 enemy types per fight); make threats readable through silhouettes and wind-ups; use verticality and landmarks for orientation and spectacle more than for moment-to-moment combat; and solve wall occlusion with dither, fade or cutaway shaders (a raycast from camera to player) or by keeping tall geometry on the camera-far side. No source gave concrete corridor-width numbers for top-down games; the numbers below are marked as inference.

### Cited Findings
- "Games in which the player can quickly traverse large distances tend to have larger arenas" than less mobile games. — [Game Developer: Level design for melee combat systems](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-for-melee-combat-systems)
- Action arenas tend to be empty, flat spaces bounded by walls. DmC's Rahni Tucker: "you bump into a waist-height wall, it's really frustrating". Height changes and collision props are used sparingly. Boundaries tell the player nothing outside matters until the fight ends. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-for-melee-combat-systems)
- Put enemies between the player and the exit; lock the exits until the fight is won (barriers, objects that break apart). For multi-wave arenas, new enemies "jump into the arena from outside the level or spawn with a visual effect". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-for-melee-combat-systems)
- Enemy variety cap: Aztez used at most **3 enemy types per fight**; "most games stay at three, with four as the maximum". Give each enemy "space for that enemy to do its best thing". Introduce new enemy types in dedicated encounters. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-for-melee-combat-systems)
- Boss arenas are designed with the boss, often mostly empty, with predefined positions that enable special AI patterns. In open areas, don't lock exits, so players can flee. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-for-melee-combat-systems)
- Doom-style fast movement with dodgeable attacks allows "huge, sprawling, often non-linear spaces", because "the player can avoid a significant amount of damage simply by moving". Cover should show where it is safe, where it is high risk, and where players can recover. — [The Level Design Book: Combat](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/combat)
- Combat-space metrics to define first: correct cover height and width, cover spacing (buffer zone), cover rules on architecture, weapon range, enemy archetypes. — [Game Developer: Level design for combat](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/level-design-for-combat) (search snippet)
- Cover placement "can encourage map flow, control pacing, provide choices and encourage strategy". You should be able to follow a player moving between cover points. — [Cover object placement tutorial](https://www.cnblogs.com/matt1985/p/13121154.html) (search snippet)
- The Ascent (isometric twin-stick shooter): cover is plentiful, and nearly any bulky block works. It is usable from any angle ("it's not like you can only use a cover from one angle"). Enemies also take cover, crouch and hide. Encounters use "a controlled random": they look random but are planned, and occasionally produce extreme groups (e.g., all snipers). — [80.lv: The secrets of game design in The Ascent](https://80.lv/articles/the-secrets-of-game-design-in-the-ascent)
- The Ascent uses verticality deliberately to make the world feel huge, with landmarks in each area for orientation ([Techraptor](https://Techraptor.net/gaming/features/ascent-neon-giant-world-interview), search snippet). Tor Frick: verticality shows both the city's vastness and how crowded and alive it is ([Wireframe](https://wireframe.raspberrypi.com/articles/exploring-the-urban-cyberpunk-world-of-the-ascent), search snippet). The game favors revisiting areas over linear "fire-and-forget" levels ([Unreal Engine interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/developer-interviews/11-person-studio-neon-giant-delivering-next-gen-passion-project-the-ascent), search snippet).
- Hyper Light Drifter (Alx Preston): keep individual enemies simple; difficulty comes from combinations ("as we add more enemies, that situation becomes exponentially more difficult"). Telegraph through "creature silhouettes and windup animations". "Combat scenarios are puzzles". Test enemy pairings (a deflecting enemy became "impossible to wrangle" next to simple ones). Zones have distinct shapes for orientation. Interconnected, vertically stacked spaces that loop back create "epiphanies". Early open areas felt aimless without locked doors and goals: "Without a specific set of goals... that stuff becomes really tiresome." — [Game Developer: How Hyper Light Drifter balances difficulty with fairness](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-hyper-light-drifter-i-balances-difficulty-with-fairness)
- Hades: early arenas are walled-in medium rooms (walls enable wall-slam damage); later arenas are larger. Asphodel uses lava islands, where terrain gating shapes enemy design. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/hades-level-design-is-less-random-than-it-seems-1845254545)
- Ruiner: described as a dash-heavy top-down action shooter ("a lot of dashing around... teleporting behind your enemies"). A search summary reported that Reikon later felt a third-person view might have served the game better than its near-isometric camera, but the original quote was not verified. — [Unreal Engine: Unmasking the motives and mayhem in RUINER](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/unmasking-the-motives-and-mayhem-in-ruiner-copy); [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/profile/66383/why-reikon-wished-it-had-made-cyberpunk-action-game-ruiner-a-little-bit-easier/) (snippet, unverified)
- Occlusion techniques in top-down and isometric games: a stencil "wall cutter" shader makes walls in front of the player transparent; raycasts from camera to player give precise occlusion tests ([Unity wall-cutter tutorial](https://sstyles93.github.io/blogs/unitytips_wallcutter)). Alternatives: a dissolve shader near the player, hiding blockers, or designing levels open enough that tall geometry never sits between camera and player ([Heist against Heretics devlog](https://purplanestudio.itch.io/heist-against-heretics/devlog/370116/top-down-view-and-character-obstruction)). Downside of hiding: "you lose a clear view of anything on the far side of the wall" ([GameDev.net: hide objects blocking player view](https://www.gamedev.net/tutorials/programming/general-and-gameplay-programming/hide-objects-blocking-player-view-r3215)). Zooming the camera in to dodge occlusion felt jarring to players ([itch.io occlusion devlog](https://itch.io/blog/522859/occlusion-culling)).
- PoE: mixing tile heights (the Act 4 Daresso Pits + Colosseum hybrid) needed solving "a slew of little technical issues" with lighting and differing tile heights. — [PoE forum interview](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1654235)
- Diablo I used bounded geometry features for readability and flavor: columns in central rooms, colonnades in halls, dividing walls 25% arches / 25% barred arches / otherwise solid; lava rivers needing bridge tiles. — [Boris the Brave: Diablo 1](https://boristhebrave.com/2019/07/14/dungeon-generation-in-diablo-1)

### Inferences
(All of the following are my own design rules of thumb. They are not sourced numbers.)
- **Corridor and room metrics (inference):** express everything in character-capsule widths (W). Traversal-only corridors: 3-4 W, so a dash or slide never clips walls. Combat corridors and chokepoints: 5-6 W, enough for one enemy to block and the player to sidestep a projectile. Small arenas: about 12-16 W across (Hades' early "medium walled-in" rooms). Large arenas: 20-30 W, scaled up if dash distance is large (the "arenas scale with mobility" rule above). At a 3/4 camera, test that the whole arena plus about 2 W of margin fits on screen at the default zoom, so off-screen enemies aren't shooting the player.
- **Cover in top-down (inference):** use two heights that read clearly from above. Low cover (blocks movement, not shots, or blocks shots only when crouched) and full cover (blocks both). Shade them distinctly (e.g., a lit top cap on full-height blocks). The Ascent's lesson: cover should work from any side (blocky, roughly square props) because enemies come from many angles. Keep cover sparse in melee-focused arenas (the "waist-high wall" frustration).
- **Height and ledges (inference):** in a 3/4 view, height reads through (a) drop shadows and ambient-occlusion edges at ledge lips, (b) visible stair or ramp silhouettes, (c) a consistent rule that higher floors are lighter or more saturated, and (d) railings on walkable edges, with none on drop-offs the player can jump down. Use height mostly as one-way drops (a shortcut down), elevated enemy perches (snipers the player must reach via stairs) and spectacle (bridges over pits, multi-level platforms visible below). Diablo I's lava-with-bridge and Hades' Asphodel islands are natural chokepoint generators.
- **LOS and flanking (inference):** in a top-down game the player sees the whole arena, so LOS is mostly about *enemy* fire lanes. Pillars and columns (Diablo I's central columns) break lanes without hiding enemies. Give every arena at least two routes around a central blocker (a loop), so the player can kite.
- **Landmarks (inference, extending The Ascent and HLD):** one oversized, unique-silhouette prop per tile or arena (reactor core, crashed dropship, giant statue), visible from neighboring tiles; plus distinct zone silhouettes and colors per biome.
- **Occlusion and camera in Godot (inference):** keep the camera at a fixed pitch (about 50-60° for 3/4 views), and author tiles so tall walls sit on the camera-far (north) side. Lower or cut "south" walls to a stub height, Diablo/Hades style. For remaining cases, raycast or shape-cast from camera to player and fade intersecting `MeshInstance3D`s with a screen-space dither or alpha shader (with a circle mask around the player). Avoid zooming the camera to solve occlusion.

### Gaps
- No sourced corridor-width, room-size or cover-height numbers for any of the listed games. No sources found on "Epic Isometric" map conventions, Helldivers' map generation, or Ruiner's or Hyper Light Drifter's room sizes. Occlusion sources are devlogs and tutorials, not shipped-game postmortems (no Diablo, Hades or The Ascent occlusion talk was found).

## Q7. Procedural + handmade hybrid techniques usable in Godot

### Takeaway
The proven hybrid stack is: **(1) a mission or flow graph** (Dormans' missions first, then space; Gungeon flows; lock-and-key), **(2) hand-authored room templates with typed door sockets** (Warframe tiles, Gungeon rooms, Edgar's polygon-plus-doors templates), **(3) a constraint-solving layout pass** (place loops first, then trees, retry on overlap), **(4) set-piece and miniset injection plus a dressing pass** (Diablo), **(5) validation and statistical tuning** (PoE's thousands of seeds), and **(6) a runtime pacing director** (Warframe's tac and influence maps). Spelunky's grid-of-templates is the simplest fallback.

### Cited Findings
- Dormans (FDG 2010): treat a level as two structures, a **mission** and a **space**, generated in separate steps with different grammars. "Generate missions first and then generate spaces to accommodate these missions". "A single generative grammar that tackles all these problems will not work." — [Dormans 2010, Adventures in Level Design (PDF)](https://pcgworkshop.com/archive/dormans2010adventures.pdf) (abstract and intro via search; full text not fetched)
- Lock-and-key: "A lock is something that stops further progress by the player, and a key something that enables that progress." Keys can be items, upgrades, passwords or event flags. **Hard** locks must be resolved as intended; **soft** locks (e.g., a tough monster) can be bypassed at risk. One key can open many locks. Mission graphs separate the high-level plan from low-level layout, which suits procedural generation. — [Boris the Brave: Lock and key dungeons](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/02/27/lock-and-key-dungeons/)
- Cyclic generation (Dormans, *Unexplored*): build the start-to-goal path plus a second path back to the start (a cycle). Insert lock-and-key sub-cycles into existing cycles. Soft locks are bypassable at a cost. — [Boris the Brave: Dungeon generation in Unexplored](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/04/10/dungeon-generation-in-unexplored/); [Game Developer: Unexplored's secret cyclic dungeon generation](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/unexplored-s-secret-cyclic-dungeon-generation-) (search snippets)
- Spelunky: a 4×4 grid of rooms. Random walk from the top row (roll 1-5: 1-2 left, 3-4 right, 5 down) with typed rooms: 1 = left+right exits; 2 = left+right+bottom; 3 = left+right+top; 0 = off-path filler with no guarantees. Downward moves force type 2, and the room after a drop must be type 2 or 3. This guarantees a solution path; off-path rooms add walled-off variety. — [Darius Kazemi: Spelunky generator lessons](https://tinysubversions.com/spelunkyGen/)
- Gungeon: flows → composites (loops or trees) → place the hardest, most central parts first, favoring tight loops and short corridors → depth-first tree placement with up to 3 retries → corridors 4-30 units. — [Boris the Brave: Gungeon](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2019/07/28/dungeon-generation-in-enter-the-gungeon/)
- Edgar (open-source graph-based generator): "You decide exactly how many rooms you want in a level and how they should be connected". Room templates are "an outline polygon and a set of available door positions". Rooms connect directly or via short corridors. There is implicit lock-and-key support. "Not suitable for large levels - we recommend less than 30 rooms." 2D grid only. — [Edgar-DotNet on GitHub](https://github.com/OndrejNepozitek/Edgar-DotNet)
- Godot ecosystem (Godot 4; check versions and licenses before use): **Edgar.Godot** converts Tiled maps and room-graph resources for runtime roguelike assembly ([Godot Asset Library](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/edit/21440)). **SimpleDungeons** generates 3D dungeons "using user defined prefab rooms" ([GitHub](https://github.com/majikayogames/SimpleDungeons)). **godot-procedural3d** defines "connectable and configurable rooms" ([listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Frodzill4%2Fgodot-procedural3d)). **Floor Architect** builds Isaac- or Moonlighter-style 2D floor layouts ([GitHub](https://www.github.com/Drejzer/FloorArchitect)). (From search results; not individually verified.)
- Diablo I injection toolkit: set pieces (1 per level), minisets (3×3 pattern replacements), theme rooms (walled rooms with preset content), tile substitutions, and regenerate when not walkable. — [Boris the Brave: Diablo 1](https://boristhebrave.com/2019/07/14/dungeon-generation-in-diablo-1)
- Warframe templates: role strings (`S C I O D E`), segment length and composition, max branch depth; a tree with no overlap; layout logged per seed. — [Warframe Wiki: Tileset](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Tileset)
- Runtime pacing beats scripted spawns in procedural levels: tac map (distance to objective drives escalation), influence map (spawn ahead of the player's line of sight), intensity curve (peak, hold, relax). — [MCV: Brewer GDC 2013](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/gdc-13-handling-ai-in-procedural-levels/)
- PoE: rooms must work rotated or flipped; restrictions block bad combinations; thousands of seeds are generated to audit monster and chest averages. — [PoE forum interview](https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1654235)
- Spy vaults (Warframe): hand-made puzzle rooms with randomized features (which lasers are active, which grates are open). — [Warframe Wiki: Spy](https://wiki.warframe.com/w/Spy)

### Inferences
(Concrete Godot pipeline proposal; inference throughout.)
1. **Mission grammar (data):** a `Resource` per mission type listing node roles and counts, e.g. Exterminate = `Start → Combat×3 → Elite → Combat×2 → Exit` with 1-2 optional branches (`Loot dead end`, `Challenge room`), and Defense = `Start → Connector → Arena(hold) → Exit`. Allow 1 loop per mission (Gungeon or Unexplored style) to cut backtracking. Place keys (switches, keycards, elites carrying a key) before locks by generating the graph backwards from the goal (lock-and-key).
2. **Room library:** each room is a `PackedScene` with root metadata (role tags: combat, arena, connector, loot, vault, boss; size class; biome) and child `Marker3D` door sockets (direction, width class). Doors snap only to matching width classes. Author connector rooms as long, thin, enemy-free hazard rooms (Gungeon's breather pattern). Make some rooms rotatable or mirrorable (PoE), and tag rooms that can't be.
3. **Layout solver:** place the loop composite first, then grow trees depth-first. Check AABB overlap with `Area3D` or a coarse occupancy grid. Retry up to N times per node, and reseed if the whole layout fails. Cap rooms per level below about 30 for a graph solver (Edgar's guidance). Bigger "missions" chain several solved segments (Warframe's segments).
4. **Variation inside rooms:** per-room "variant groups" (Diablo minisets and Warframe's randomized vault features): toggle props, cover layouts, hazards and loot spots via seeded random selection of child nodes. This multiplies a 30-room library cheaply.
5. **Validation:** after assembly, bake `NavigationRegion3D` at runtime and check that every door, objective and extraction point is reachable from the start. Check minimum combat area and maximum walk distance. Batch-run 1,000+ seeds headless and dump CSV stats (rooms, enemies, chests, path length), as GGG does.
6. **Pacing director:** a singleton tracks distance-to-objective (a Dijkstra map over the room graph), player heading, and an intensity value (damage taken + kills). Spawn ahead of the player in unseen rooms, ramp toward the objective, and force relax windows after peaks.
7. **Reward-preview exits:** when an arena is cleared, label each outgoing door with a reward type rolled from the mission's pool (Hades).
8. **Simplest fallback:** for early prototypes, a Spelunky-style N×M grid of fixed-size room cells with typed exits guarantees a solution path with almost no solver code.
